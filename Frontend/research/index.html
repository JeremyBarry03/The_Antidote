The models, the data,
and what happens next.

At a glance

The Antidote estimates when a ship will cross an analytical harbor gate in Hampton Roads. The historical replay compares quadratic regression, ordinary XGBoost, and Antidote’s XGBoost model with conditional input repairs as AIS reports are delayed, missing, noisy, or biased. When an issue is detected, a human records its error type; those reviewed examples are saved and feed the next model-training cycle. The intended user is a port logistics coordinator deciding whether an arrival-dependent plan needs verification.

This overview brings together the full-March nominal comparison with attack and mitigation results. It explains how reports are disrupted, how the models respond, and how to interpret the findings.

The problem

The intended user is a port logistics coordinator deciding whether to keep an arrival-dependent plan or verify it before committing resources. A late or unstable estimate can cause avoidable waiting, premature staffing, or repeated replanning. The prototype supplies an estimate and evidence for that decision; it does not allocate a berth, issue navigation instructions, or control a vessel. The historical replay supplies the interactive view of ships, incoming reports, data changes, and forecasts. Here, we explain the inputs and decisions behind those forecasts.

Our outcome is an analytical harbor-gate crossing, not docking, cargo delivery, or an official port-arrival timestamp. A qualifying east-to-west crossing intersects longitude λg=−76.3∘\lambda_g=-76.3^\circ between latitudes 36.95∘36.95^\circ and 37.05∘37.05^\circ. Consecutive original observations must bracket it within 120 seconds. If the two longitudes are λ0,λ1\lambda_0,\lambda_1, the reference is interpolated as α=λg−λ0λ1−λ0,T*=t0+α(t1−t0).\alpha=\frac{\lambda_g-\lambda_0}{\lambda_1-\lambda_0},\qquad T^*=t_0+\alpha(t_1-t_0). Interpolation assumes approximately linear motion inside the bracket. Sampling, position error, and uncertain destination intent remain limitations of the reference. The original bracket is fixed before introducing faults and never becomes a repair input. Cohort membership requires an observed qualifying crossing, so results cannot establish performance for unresolved approaches or vessels that never arrive.

We assess forecasts using MAE, MSE, 95th-percentile absolute error, prediction coverage, review burden, and condition-level results. Together, these measures show average and tail performance, forecast availability, and intervention needs.

The data and evaluation window

Source and target

The replay serves frozen A, B, and nominal-only C models across all 31 days of March 2024. Source and model hashes record the data lineage and configuration.

The March source is NOAA’s cleaned broadcast-point AIS product, selected for Hampton Roads and filtered to cargo and tanker reports. The bundle records the March source checksum and model/code signatures. Its target is an interpolated analytical gate crossing, not verified docking or official arrival. NOAA’s metadata describes collection and processing limits.1

Shared preprocessing selects cargo and tanker reports before simulated masking, parses UTC times, treats invalid navigation values as missing, and removes exact duplicate measurements consistently across models. MMSI identifies repeated vessel observations; it is not an independent observation count. Source archives already involve processing and incomplete receiver coverage, so “original” means unchanged by our simulator, not error-free ground truth.

In the live replay, the reporting-gap control withholds reports for a chosen interval; it does not deliver old messages later. A separate saved stress curve tests delayed delivery. Neither substitutes for measured receiver latency.

The solution

Quadratic regression: a structured comparator

Model A expands the base features with degree-two polynomial terms and fits ridge regression. With feature expansion ϕ(x)\phi(x), it minimizes ∑iwi(yi−β𝖳ϕ(xi))2+λ‖β‖22.\sum_i w_i\bigl(y_i-\beta^\mathsf T\phi(x_i)\bigr)^2 +\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2. The base representation includes local east/north position, current speed, sine and cosine of course, recent mean speed and position, speed trend, history support, and explicit cargo/tanker/unknown-class flags. Circular course encoding avoids treating 359∘359^\circ and 1∘1^\circ as nearly a full rotation apart. Polynomial interactions provide a compact nonlinear comparator; regularization limits coefficient growth. This model is inspectable, but it can extrapolate poorly during waiting, turning, or unfamiliar approach patterns.

Ordinary XGBoost: a nonlinear comparator

Model B uses gradient-boosted regression trees on the same base feature contract and nominal training inputs. Predictions combine sequential tree contributions, f(x)=∑m=1Mhm(x)f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}h_m(x). XGBoost supplies the nonlinear learner; it is the AI capability under evaluation, rather than merely a tool used to author the website.2 Missing numeric features pass through an imputer fitted on training data. Unknown vessel class has its own flags and is not silently turned into cargo.

Both A and B predict remaining duration from the latest observation. Their absolute arrival prediction is T̂A,B=tobs+fA,B(x)\widehat T_{A,B}=t_{\mathrm{obs}}+f_{A,B}(x). Subtracting the current decision clock yields the remaining time now. An older observation can retain the same absolute forecast while its remaining duration shrinks; changing a display clock does not refresh the underlying evidence.

Antidote: degradation-aware learning and conditional repairs

Model C is also XGBoost. It adds robust motion summaries and observable freshness/quality signals: speed quantiles and variability, turn and course dispersion, a position-history velocity fit, report age, gap length, missing-field fractions, occupied minute bins, and disagreement between reported and position-derived motion. It predicts from the decision clock, T̂C=t+g(z)\widehat T_C=t+g(z). The model served in the current comparison is the nominal-only C artifact: it was fitted on original reports, without injected deterioration. The separate augmented C remains an experimental artifact and is not served. Conditional repairs are added by the dashboard at inference time.

The trained guard limits report influence by forming one representative per UTC minute using medians and a circular course summary. It screens invalid coordinates and motion exceeding 60 knots across short gaps of at most 300 seconds. Minute binning limits the contribution of a prolific feed to history summaries; it does not make a biased majority within a minute trustworthy. Exact deduplication is shared with the ordinary comparators, so it cannot be credited exclusively to Antidote.

The current dashboard adds conditional repair strategies to this frozen C model. These are separate from its learned guard. The code keeps raw A/B inputs, original quality signals, support, labels, and clocks intact while changing only permitted derived C inputs. Training uses squared-error regression; depth and learning rate are selected using approach-balanced development MAE. The configured search spans depths 2, 4, and 6, learning rates 0.03 and 0.1, up to 500 boosting rounds, and early stopping after 30 rounds. Choosing a model by MAE does not ensure its MSE or worst errors improve.

Degradation scenarios

The replay studies accidental deterioration at inference time: a frozen model version receives worse reports. No live AIS broadcasts are altered, and a reviewed label does not silently change the model serving that replay. Instead, reviewed cases are retained for the next versioned training cycle. Although controls are called attacks in code, injected changes stand in for outages, unavailable telemetry, miscalibration, and navigation faults; they are not evidence of malicious activity in NOAA records.

Fault Controlled change Mechanism affecting the forecast
Reporting gap Withhold all reports in a fixed interval beginning ten minutes into an approach. Old position and motion remain the latest evidence; data age increases while the ship continues moving.
Missing speed Mask selected SOG values. Current speed, recent summaries, and missingness evidence change. Zero speed remains a legitimate observation.
Missing course Mask selected COG values. Direction and turning features lose support; numerical imputation may follow a different tree path.
Missing vessel class Mask selected type reports. The model loses cargo/tanker context; unknown remains a distinct state.
Speed drift Add a slowly varying bounded speed bias. A persistent underestimate can resemble slower progress and shift predicted duration.
Location drift Accumulate a bounded coordinate displacement. Distance to the gate and position-derived motion become biased together.
Sensor deterioration Add zero-mean Gaussian noise to SOG and COG. Instantaneous errors propagate into direction, speed variability, and nonlinear tree decisions.
Position jump Displace selected reports along a seeded direction. Apparent progress or implausible motion changes position and quality features.

The saved March graph comparisons use 25% of each control’s configured maximum: a 15-minute reporting gap and 25% for each other control. The replay sliders affect live replay inputs independently; the graph selector uses the saved fixed-strength scenarios described in the results section.

Faults compose in a fixed order: gap, speed drift, location drift, noise, position jumps, and missing fields. Missing-field and position-jump selectors share a deterministic report hash and seed. Equal-severity selections therefore overlap subject to field eligibility; they are not independent dropout masks. Modification logs are constructed separately. Audit labels, original altered values, and future crossing outcomes are exposed to the audience only after inference.

Repairs, warnings, and deferral

Reconstructing missing motion and noisy sensors

When positions are sufficiently consistent, a short local track can estimate motion. For local east/north position 𝒑i\mathbf p_i and times centred around t‾\bar t, the least-squares velocity is 𝒗̂=∑i(ti−t‾)(𝒑i−𝒑‾)∑i(ti−t‾)2,ŝ=‖𝒗̂‖0.514444,θ̂=atan2⁡(v̂E,v̂N).\widehat{\mathbf v}= \frac{\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)(\mathbf p_i-\bar{\mathbf p})} {\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2},\qquad \widehat s=\frac{\lVert\widehat{\mathbf v}\rVert}{0.514444},\qquad \widehat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\widehat v_E,\widehat v_N). Positions are in metres and time is in seconds; ŝ\widehat s is therefore in knots. Course is converted to degrees and wrapped around the circle. A stable trajectory can support estimates for missing speed and course independently. Both use original received positions; one reconstructed sensor does not become evidence for the other. Each historical correction uses only information available when that particular report arrived, preventing later reports from backfilling an earlier estimate.

The Gaussian-noise strategy checks a five-minute track with at least three distinct reports, at least 60 seconds of span, no gap above 180 seconds, and fit residual at most 50 metres. Additional speed, turn, and plausibility checks reject unsuitable references. It replaces a supported reading when its speed differs by more than 2 knots or its moving-course difference exceeds 15∘15^\circ. This is guarded replacement, not a fitted blend of the reported and estimated readings. A smoother track is still only a conditional reference, not independent ground truth.

Detecting persistent bias

Speed drift is checked against position-supported speed. For disagreement rk=vk−ŝkr_k=v_k-\widehat s_k, the detector forms a time-aware exponentially weighted estimate, ek=(1−ρk)ek−1+ρkrk,ρk=1−exp⁡(−Δtk/τ),τ=2 minutes.e_k=(1-\rho_k)e_{k-1}+\rho_k r_k,\qquad \rho_k=1-\exp(-\Delta t_k/\tau),\qquad \tau=2\text{ minutes}. The provisional rule requires same-direction disagreement across at least three reports and two minutes, with |ek|>0.5|e_k|>0.5 knots. Reference acceleration, track fit, and turning checks can prevent reconstruction. These conditions reduce impulsive replacements but also reject legitimate manoeuvres; the screen finds limited repair availability for speed drift. The active development branch uses Kalman filtering for sensor-drift mitigation. The equations below document the EWMA-based implementation currently in this main-branch overview. When persistent disagreement passes the gate, the reported speed is replaced by the position-derived least-squares speed; otherwise the reading remains as reported or is deferred for review.

Location drift compares each observed displacement with the distance implied by the received speed and course. With local position p in metres, velocity v in metres per minute, and elapsed time Δt in minutes, the residual displacement is dk=(pk−pk−1)−vk+vk−12Δt,ek=(1−ρk)ek−1+ρkdkΔt,ρk=1−exp(−Δt/τ).\mathbf d_k=(\mathbf p_k-\mathbf p_{k-1})-\frac{\mathbf v_k+\mathbf v_{k-1}}{2}\Delta t,\quad \mathbf e_k=(1-\rho_k)\mathbf e_{k-1}+\rho_k\frac{\mathbf d_k}{\Delta t},\quad \rho_k=1-\exp(-\Delta t/\tau). The vector EWMA uses a two-minute smoothing time constant and flags persistent mismatch; it cannot determine which input is wrong. The provisional trigger requires at least three reports over two minutes, EWMA magnitude above 8 metres per minute, accumulated disagreement above 30 metres, and directionally aligned residuals within 45 degrees; these thresholds still need development calibration. In the active replay, the response is a warning and a request to verify position. A separate experimental correction projects forward from an explicit, time-available position anchor by integrating trusted SOG/COG, using trapezoidal steps, for no more than five minutes: p^(t)=panchor+∫tanchortvnav(u)du.\widehat{\mathbf p}(t)=\mathbf p_{anchor}+\int_{t_{anchor}}^t\mathbf v_{nav}(u)\,du The anchors in that experiment are simulated from original AIS, so this is an oracle-reference study, not an independent operational feed. Coherent corruption of both position and motion may evade the consistency checks.

Fallbacks, metadata, and combined faults

The reporting-gap strategy detects silence from observed cadence and does not fabricate reports. Position-jump detection supplies navigation-consistency warnings and does not repair coordinates. Missing class can be carried forward only from consistent recent received metadata for the same MMSI; conflicts remain unknown and require review.

Position faults pause position-based navigation repairs. Noise plus speed drift pauses their overlapping sensor corrections; class recovery can continue independently. The current fleet policy selects permitted reference assumptions from the simulated fault families enabled in the controls. It is not blind automatic fault isolation. Pausing avoids asserting an unsupported correction, but it is not evidence that the forecast has recovered. A production system would need independently justified reference trust and an evaluated fallback policy.

Staleness

At each decision clock, the model receives reports only after their simulated delivery time, and report age continues increasing until a newer measurement arrives. The live replay’s gap control withholds reports, while a separate saved stress curve recomputes forecasts under delayed delivery; these are distinct scenarios. Nominal delivery is assumed instantaneous, so neither is a measurement of real receiver latency. The baseline table above gives the full-March reference; stress graphs show how forecasts move as delayed evidence accumulates.

Low-quality data and source volume

The replay separately exposes missing fields, noisy or drifting sensors, and position jumps, then allows these conditions to be combined. Fault definitions and affected fields are shown above. Fault strength and source volume are different questions: strength changes how unreliable an affected report is, while source volume changes how many reports come from that source. A fixed-volume prevalence test varies the fraction of unreliable reports while holding the total report count constant. Repetition tests ask whether repeated reports from one source gain influence; total-volume tests ask whether adding reports changes that influence when the unreliable share stays fixed. Exact duplicates are removed by shared preprocessing for every model, so only nonidentical repetition can test whether a source gains weight through additional observations.

Volume matters when downstream summaries give a more prolific source more influence. Here, minute-level medians and bounded history summaries limit that influence, but a biased majority within a time bin can still move its representative value. The current March graph bundle instead uses a fixed 25% fault strength on six sampled approaches.

The single-fault objectives build on the repair comparisons above; the broader objectives extend the comparison across clean, stale, low-quality, and combined conditions.

Single-fault objectives: target about 5.4% lower ETA MAE for missing speed, 4.2% for missing course, and 0.3% for missing vessel class, each versus its own attacked C baseline. Retain the observed 100% forecast coverage. These percentages describe separate conditions and should not be added together.

Broader one-month objectives:

  • Nominal: reduce approach-weighted MAE by 12% versus current Model B, from 6.643 to about 5.846 minutes, while reducing MSE by at least 10% and retaining 100% coverage.

  • Staleness: when 25% of reports are withheld for ten minutes, reduce MAE by at least 20% versus the same frozen XGBoost model without repair, with at least 95% coverage.

  • Source volume: when nonidentical reports from an unreliable source are doubled, keep MAE within 10% of nominal and improve MAE by at least 20% versus the same stressed model without repair.

  • Combined faults: across the eight controls at 25% strength, lower MAE by at least 15% and MSE by at least 10% versus the same attacked cases with repairs disabled, retain at least 95% coverage, and limit clean-feed MAE regression to 2%.

  • Across time and feedback: reproduce the direction of the combined-fault gain across all 31 March days and three paired seeds; after one review-informed training cycle, lower MAE by at least 10% on later repeat-fault cases, cut false-positive review requests on clean reports by 20%, and retain at least 95% sensitivity to injected faults.

Where humans are involved

When Antidote detects a data-quality issue or an estimate that warrants attention, the reviewer sees the received timestamp, affected fields, diagnostic evidence, and available forecasts. The reviewer identifies the error type when the evidence supports one (for example, a stale report, missing field, sensor noise, speed drift, location drift, or position jump); uncertain cases remain unresolved rather than being forced into a category. The reviewed label and its evidence are saved as a feedback record for later model training. The reviewer does not overwrite the original AIS observation or invent an arrival outcome.

For a reviewed event ii, let xix_i be the evidence available at the decision time and let ziz_i be its human-adjudicated error type. The feedback store retains the event context and model version alongside (xi,zi)(x_i,z_i). A later training cycle adds these reviewed examples to the versioned training set, so a candidate can learn which observable patterns correspond to recurring error types: ϕ̂v+1=arg⁡minϕ[−∑i∈ℱvlogpϕ(zi∣xi)].\widehat\phi_{v+1}=\arg\min_{\phi}\left[-\sum_{i\in\mathcal F_v}\log p_{\phi}(z_i\mid x_i)\right]. Here ℱv\mathcal F_v contains only reviewed feedback available to training version v+1v+1. If a verified crossing outcome yiy_i is also available, its example can contribute to the ETA objective, ℓETA(fθ(xi),yi)\ell_{\mathrm{ETA}}(f_{\theta}(x_i),y_i); an error-type label by itself is not treated as ETA ground truth. This creates a repeatable improvement loop: detect, review, save, retrain, and validate. New candidates are evaluated as versioned models before they replace the frozen replay model, so improvement is the goal of each cycle rather than a guaranteed outcome of every retrain.

An operator should inspect the received timestamp, altered-field evidence, active warning, and available estimates before accepting an unreliable arrival-dependent plan. Simulator audit highlighting explains the experiment and is not used as a human error label or a deployable fault detector. A position inconsistency calls for checking an independent navigation or port source. Antidote exposes conditional estimates without overwriting the displayed received measurements; correction of a source report belongs to the accountable data provider or a separately verified input workflow.

The latest status policy separates an unavailable optional noise/drift reconstruction from an actionable review request. Turning or accelerating can invalidate a simple track fit without proving a fault. Such a case shows “Estimate unavailable” while preserving the input evidence. Detected inconsistencies, blocked corrections, missing required support, and other existing review warnings remain. This follows the general principle that alerts should prompt a useful human action.3

Human-reviewed error types and their supporting context are saved for retraining. These labels provide feedback about observed failure modes, but do not by themselves measure alert precision or operator workload.

Data governance and limitations

The reviewed NOAA product is publicly accessible; its metadata lists coastal and ocean planning as the use constraint. Public access alone is not blanket authorization for redistribution or operational navigation. Retain attribution, preserve the selected product’s metadata, and verify applicable terms before publishing raw subsets or acquiring a live feed. The serving bundle includes model artifacts, a March replay cache, and hashes; source training CSVs are not included in the tracked bundle. The 2023 acquisition record still needs source URLs, retrieval dates, geographic filters, completeness notes, and counts tied to the exact training view.

MMSIs and precise historical tracks identify vessels and can expose operational patterns. We use identity only for grouping and display, without attempting to identify crew, enrich operator profiles, or join unrelated personal information. A live pilot would need explicit access, retention, logging, and sharing rules. Geography, receiving coverage, cargo/tanker selection, repeat vessels, and exclusion of unresolved approaches all constrain representation. Accuracy should be audited by vessel class, forecast horizon, data support, and previously seen vessels before broader claims.

The feedback records retain only the information needed to interpret and reproduce a reviewed event: the relevant input snapshot, assigned error type, model version, review state, and a verified crossing outcome when available. Simulator-generated fault flags remain experiment metadata and do not become human labels. In a proposed pilot, the data steward owns provenance, access, retention, and permitted use; the model lead owns versioned retraining, validation, drift monitoring, and rollback; an operations reviewer owns adjudication and escalation. The submitting team remains accountable for claims and source review. AI assistance was used for implementation and drafting. A language model is not part of the ETA inference path. This overview describes a research prototype, not a certified navigation aid, deployment approval, or a formal human-readiness rating.

Next steps

The present decision is iterate. The product works as a historical replay, and the nominal March comparison has a saved results and verification record. Current attack evidence is sampled, fixed-strength, and insufficient to establish full-cohort mitigation value or operator readiness.

  1. Model lead: finish the all-eligible-2023 training protocol using time-ordered model selection within 2023. Freeze model and repair choices before the final March scoring pass; disclose the existing March exposure and treat a later period as confirmation. Using the existing offline CPU workflow and matching authorized source files, evaluate every March clock, multiple fault seeds, isolated and combined conditions, coverage, MSE, tail errors, and the same-C repair ablation. Repeat source-volume controls and investigate any large-error cases using development data only; no live AIS integration is needed for this study.

  2. Data steward: complete the 2023 and March acquisition manifests, including source URLs, dates, geographic filters, checksums, rights, and retained approach counts. Verify redistribution terms and obtain an independent position reference before making coordinate-recovery claims.

  3. Demo and operations reviewers: guide judges through clean, stale, low-quality, combined, repaired, and failed examples. Then run an explicitly exploratory oversight walkthrough that records human effort separately from model latency. Follow the institution’s required approval process before any participant study; a peer walkthrough alone cannot establish operator readiness.

The checkpoint is a versioned report showing nominal and degraded accuracy, MAE/MSE and large-error behavior, output coverage, alert misses, review burden, and resource use. Revisit a controlled pilot only if the reach goals hold without unacceptable tail-error or workload tradeoffs, a port partner approves data access, and accountable roles and non-actuating boundaries are in place. Stop or narrow the scope if those conditions fail.

For a ten-minute judge walkthrough, spend one minute on the port-planning decision and crossing reference, three minutes on the clean replay and nominal comparison, three minutes showing a reporting gap and a sensor-quality fault, two minutes on Antidote’s repair and a case where it cannot safely correct, then one minute on the measured limits and next evidence needed.

Its results are reported above as the current full-March nominal comparison. The attack graphs are saved in Frontend/tracking/data/scenarios.json; their metadata records the six selected approaches, fixed strength, source/model/code hashes, and scalar parity checks.

The serving bundle contains the model artifacts needed for replay; the website does not train on startup. Full setup and preparation commands are in the repository’s frontend guide and model guide. From Data_Science, the normal service command is:

python -m Optimized_Model serve --port 8765

The saved March comparison bundle can be checked with python experiments/static_graphs/export.py --verify-only. Its source/model/code signatures and scalar forecast checks support consistency with the serving path, not operational correctness.
