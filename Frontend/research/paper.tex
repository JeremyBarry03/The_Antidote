\begin{abstract}
The Antidote estimates when a ship will cross a harbor gate in Hampton Roads. We compare quadratic regression, ordinary XGBoost, and Antidote: a degradation-aware XGBoost model with conditional data repairs. The replay shows how their forecasts change as incoming AIS reports become stale, incomplete, noisy, or biased.

Our evaluation protocol trains on all of 2023 and tests on March 2024. The saved benchmarks available here come from an earlier March-training/April-evaluation setup and are labeled preliminary. They show useful recovery for several missing-field and noise conditions, alongside large-error failures and little extra recovery when all eight faults are stacked. The sections below explain the inputs, model choices, fault mechanisms, repairs, and limits behind those results.
\end{abstract}

\section{What we predict}
The intended user is a port logistics coordinator deciding whether to keep an arrival-dependent plan or verify it before committing resources. A late or unstable estimate can cause avoidable waiting, premature staffing, or repeated replanning. The prototype supplies an estimate and evidence for that decision; it does not allocate a berth, issue navigation instructions, or control a vessel. The \href{/tracking/}{historical replay} supplies the interactive view of ships, incoming reports, data changes, and forecasts. Here, we explain the inputs and decisions behind those forecasts.

Our outcome is an \emph{analytical harbor-gate crossing}, not docking, cargo delivery, or an official port-arrival timestamp. A qualifying east-to-west crossing intersects longitude $\lambda_g=-76.3^\circ$ between latitudes $36.95^\circ$ and $37.05^\circ$. Consecutive original observations must bracket it within 120 seconds. If the two longitudes are $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$, the reference is interpolated as
\[
 \alpha=\frac{\lambda_g-\lambda_0}{\lambda_1-\lambda_0},\qquad
 T^*=t_0+\alpha(t_1-t_0).
\]
Interpolation assumes approximately linear motion inside the bracket. Sampling, position error, and uncertain destination intent remain limitations of the reference. The original bracket is fixed before introducing faults and never becomes a repair input. Cohort membership requires an observed qualifying crossing, so results cannot establish performance for unresolved approaches or vessels that never arrive.

The project's initial accuracy targets are at least a 10\% reduction in degraded-condition MAE relative to ordinary XGBoost, with no more than a 5\% nominal MAE penalty. These are research decision thresholds, not safety tolerances. Output availability, large-error behavior, uncertainty, and review effort must also be examined; an average improvement alone is insufficient.

\section{The data behind the predictions}
\subsection{Where the reports come from}
The study uses Hampton Roads AIS reports from the complete 2023 calendar year for training and March 2024 for testing. The 2023 source files, acquisition manifest, and fitted model artifacts are not present in the repository material reviewed for this overview. Their source URLs, retrieval dates, geographic filters, checksums, and retained approach counts must accompany the final run; a full-year date range alone does not establish complete receiver coverage.

The available preliminary artifacts instead use local March and April 2024 subsets. The April acquisition manifest identifies NOAA's cleaned broadcast-point GeoParquet product, records daily source URLs and checksums, and dates retrieval to 20 October 2026. Its selected rectangle is $[-76.6,-75.8]^\circ$ longitude by $[36.7,37.2]^\circ$ latitude. The merged April subset contains 3,229,675 records over 1--21 April. The March input is identified by its local filename and checksum; an equivalent March acquisition timestamp is not present in the reviewed source manifest. These records document the earlier experiments, not a completed 2023 acquisition.

Shared preprocessing selects cargo and tanker reports before simulated masking, parses UTC times, treats invalid navigation values as missing, and removes exact duplicate measurements consistently across models. The prepared-source audit records 600,738 March and 393,613 April cargo/tanker rows before exact-duplicate removal. MMSI identifies repeated vessel observations; it is not an independent observation count. Source archives already involve processing and incomplete receiver coverage, so ``original'' means unchanged by our simulator, not error-free ground truth. NOAA's metadata describes collection and processing limits.\footnote{\href{https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/73064}{NOAA, Nationwide Automatic Identification System 2024.}}

\subsection{Training on 2023, testing on March 2024}
The temporal separation is the study's central data contract. All three final models fit on 2023 data; March 2024 is the common test period. It is not an 80/10/10 random row split. Model selection and any threshold or calibration fitting must use only time-ordered partitions within 2023, with complete approaches kept together. After settings are fixed, the final estimators can be refitted on all eligible 2023 approaches. The March test labels must not choose features, repair thresholds, hyperparameters, or an attack setting that looks favourable.

The saved preliminary pipeline trained on 1--19 March 2024, developed on 20--24 March, explored 25--31 March, calibrated on 1--7 April, and evaluated 8--21 April. Its April cohort contains 112 approaches, 110 distinct vessels, and 2,402 scheduled clocks. These older artifacts remain useful for examining mechanisms and failures, but their metrics cannot be presented as results from training on 2023 and testing on March 2024.

For report $r_i$, let $t_i$ be its measurement time and $d_i$ its simulated delivery time. At decision clock $t$, inference receives only
\[
 \mathcal D(t)=\{r_i:t_i\leq t,\ d_i\leq t\},\qquad
 a(t)=t-\max_{r_i\in\mathcal D(t)}t_i.
\]
The second quantity is the age of the latest measurement. Nominal delivery is instantaneous by assumption; it is not a measurement of actual receiver latency. Forecasts are scheduled every five minutes after at least ten minutes of an approach. Features summarize the ten minutes ending at the latest available observation, while age continues to increase at the decision clock. This preserves what an operator could know as time advances.

\section{How the models differ}
\subsection{Quadratic regression: a structured comparator}
Model A expands the base features with degree-two polynomial terms and fits ridge regression. With feature expansion $\phi(x)$, it minimizes
\[
 \sum_i w_i\bigl(y_i-\beta^\mathsf T\phi(x_i)\bigr)^2
 +\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2.
\]
The base representation includes local east/north position, current speed, sine and cosine of course, recent mean speed and position, speed trend, history support, and explicit cargo/tanker/unknown-class flags. Circular course encoding avoids treating $359^\circ$ and $1^\circ$ as nearly a full rotation apart. Polynomial interactions provide a compact nonlinear comparator; regularization limits coefficient growth. This model is inspectable, but it can extrapolate poorly during waiting, turning, or unfamiliar approach patterns.

\subsection{Ordinary XGBoost: a nonlinear comparator}
Model B uses gradient-boosted regression trees on the same base feature contract and nominal training inputs. Predictions combine sequential tree contributions, $f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}h_m(x)$. XGBoost supplies the nonlinear learner; it is the AI capability under evaluation, rather than merely a tool used to author the website.\footnote{\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02754}{Chen and Guestrin, XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System, 2016.}} Missing numeric features pass through an imputer fitted on training data. Unknown vessel class has its own flags and is not silently turned into cargo.

Both A and B predict remaining duration from the latest observation. Their absolute arrival prediction is $\widehat T_{A,B}=t_{\mathrm{obs}}+f_{A,B}(x)$. Subtracting the current decision clock yields the remaining time now. An older observation can retain the same absolute forecast while its remaining duration shrinks; changing a display clock does not refresh the underlying evidence.

\subsection{Antidote: degradation-aware learning and conditional repairs}
Model C is also XGBoost. It adds robust motion summaries and observable freshness/quality signals: speed quantiles and variability, turn and course dispersion, a position-history velocity fit, report age, gap length, missing-field fractions, occupied minute bins, and disagreement between reported and position-derived motion. It predicts from the decision clock, $\widehat T_C=t+g(z)$. The saved preliminary model was trained with nominal, delayed, position-offset, and combined examples. The 2023 refit should preserve this declared training recipe or record any changes before the March run. The saved model is not trained on every new eight-control combination or on every reconstructed-input pattern.

The trained guard limits report influence by forming one representative per UTC minute using medians and a circular course summary. It screens invalid coordinates and motion exceeding 60 knots across short gaps of at most 300 seconds. Minute binning limits the contribution of a prolific feed to history summaries; it does not make a biased majority within a minute trustworthy. Exact deduplication is shared with the ordinary comparators, so it cannot be credited exclusively to Antidote.

The current dashboard adds conditional repair strategies to this frozen C model. These are separate from its learned guard. The code keeps raw A/B inputs, original quality signals, support, labels, and clocks intact while changing only permitted derived C inputs. Training uses squared-error regression; depth and learning rate are selected using approach-balanced development MAE. The configured search spans depths 2, 4, and 6, learning rates 0.03 and 0.1, up to 500 boosting rounds, and early stopping after 30 rounds. Choosing a model by MAE does not ensure its MSE or worst errors improve.

\section{How we stress the data}
The case studies accidental deterioration at \emph{inference time}: frozen models receive worse reports. No live AIS broadcasts are altered and no online model retraining occurs. Although controls are called attacks in code, injected changes stand in for outages, unavailable telemetry, miscalibration, and navigation faults; they are not evidence of malicious activity in NOAA records.

For illustration, the navigation perturbations can be written as
\[
 \widetilde v_i=\max\{0,v_i+b_v(t_i)+\epsilon_i\},\qquad
 \widetilde{\mathbf p}_i=\mathbf p_i+\mathbf b_p(t_i)+J_i\mathbf u_i.
\]
Here $b_v$ is persistent speed bias, $\epsilon_i$ is random speed noise, $\mathbf b_p$ is coordinate drift, and $J_i\mathbf u_i$ is a position jump. The simulator also wraps noisy course into $[0,360)^\circ$. Drift and noise differ: averaging can reduce zero-mean jitter under appropriate assumptions, while a persistent bias generally survives averaging.

The representative pair screen uses 50\% non-gap sliders and a 15-minute gap. At that setting, speed drift has a nominal rate of $-0.05$ knots/minute, capped at 1.5 knots; location drift has a nominal rate of 10 metres/minute, capped at 926 metres. Drift rates include the configured seeded variation. Noise standard deviations are 1 knot and $5^\circ$. Position jumps select half the reports and use a 2,000-metre mean magnitude with 200-metre standard deviation. The severe stack uses 100\% sliders and a 30-minute gap.

Faults compose in a fixed order: gap, speed drift, location drift, noise, position jumps, and missing fields. Missing-field and position-jump selectors share a deterministic report hash and seed. Equal-severity selections therefore overlap subject to field eligibility; they are not independent dropout masks. Modification logs are constructed separately. Audit labels, original altered values, and future crossing outcomes are exposed to the audience only after inference.

\section{How Antidote responds}
\subsection{Reconstructing missing motion and noisy sensors}
When positions are sufficiently consistent, a short local track can estimate motion. For local east/north position $\mathbf p_i$ and times centred around $\bar t$, the least-squares velocity is
\[
 \widehat{\mathbf v}=
 \frac{\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)(\mathbf p_i-\bar{\mathbf p})}
      {\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2},\qquad
 \widehat s=\frac{\lVert\widehat{\mathbf v}\rVert}{0.514444},\qquad
 \widehat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\widehat v_E,\widehat v_N).
\]
Positions are in metres and time is in seconds; $\widehat s$ is therefore in knots. Course is converted to degrees and wrapped around the circle. A stable trajectory can support estimates for missing speed and course independently. Both use original received positions; one reconstructed sensor does not become evidence for the other. Each historical correction uses only information available when that particular report arrived, preventing later reports from backfilling an earlier estimate.

The Gaussian-noise strategy checks a five-minute track with at least three distinct reports, at least 60 seconds of span, no gap above 180 seconds, and fit residual at most 50 metres. Additional speed, turn, and plausibility checks reject unsuitable references. It replaces a supported reading when its speed differs by more than 2 knots or its moving-course difference exceeds $15^\circ$. This is \emph{guarded replacement}, not a fitted blend of the reported and estimated readings. A smoother track is still only a conditional reference, not independent ground truth.

\subsection{Detecting persistent bias}
Speed drift is checked against position-supported speed. For disagreement $r_k=v_k-\widehat s_k$, the detector forms a time-aware exponentially weighted estimate,
\[
 e_k=(1-\rho_k)e_{k-1}+\rho_k r_k,\qquad
 \rho_k=1-\exp(-\Delta t_k/\tau),\qquad \tau=2\text{ minutes}.
\]
The provisional rule requires same-direction disagreement across at least three reports and two minutes, with $|e_k|>0.5$ knots. Reference acceleration, track fit, and turning checks can prevent reconstruction. These conditions reduce impulsive replacements but also reject legitimate manoeuvres; the screen finds limited repair availability for speed drift.

Location drift compares observed displacement with motion implied by received SOG/COG. Persistent displacement disagreement can justify verification, but it cannot identify the true coordinates by itself. The dashboard therefore warns rather than correcting location. A separate experimental mode projects from explicit, time-available position anchors for at most five minutes and requires trusted navigation. Those anchors are simulated from original AIS in the experiment, so they are an oracle-reference study, not an independent operational feed. Coherent corruption of both position and motion may evade these consistency checks.

\subsection{Fallbacks, metadata, and combined faults}
The reporting-gap strategy detects silence from observed cadence and does not fabricate reports. Position-jump detection supplies navigation-consistency warnings and does not repair coordinates. Missing class can be carried forward only from consistent recent received metadata for the same MMSI; conflicts remain unknown and require review.

Position faults pause position-based navigation repairs. Noise plus speed drift pauses their overlapping sensor corrections; class recovery can continue independently. The current fleet policy selects permitted reference assumptions from the simulated fault families enabled in the controls. It is not blind automatic fault isolation. Pausing avoids asserting an unsupported correction, but it is not evidence that the forecast has recovered. A production system would need independently justified reference trust and an evaluated fallback policy.

\section{What the results show}
\subsection{Keeping comparisons fair}
For the 2023-trained study, evaluate every qualifying March approach on its original scheduled clocks. Compare A, B, C with additional repairs disabled, and full Antidote using the same fixed reference outcomes, received-report progression, fault realization, and scheduled-case denominator. Report the final approach, vessel, and clock counts from that run rather than carrying forward counts from April. No completed result for this training/test protocol is recorded in the reviewed artifacts.

The available preliminary screen uses \texttt{vessel\_class\_v2}, fault seed 4201, and four original scheduled clocks per April approach: first, first at or after 20 minutes from approach start, middle, and last. Clock selection does not use forecast error. The 448 clocks cover 112 approaches and 110 vessels. Original records, eight single faults, all 28 pairs, and two all-eight stacks produce 39 settings, 17,472 paired replay cases, and 69,888 forecasts across A, B, C with extra repairs disabled, and full Antidote. This is a representative screen, not the full 2,402-clock schedule.

Within a comparison, models receive reports available at the same clock and are scored against the same original crossing. The C-only comparison isolates the incremental repair effect while retaining the identical trained C model and guard. Ordinary B and quadratic A use the same attacked raw reports. The four-condition structure of original, stale-only, quality-only, and combined inputs is retained; the interactive timeline supplies the corresponding ordered report progression. Gap withholding in this screen differs from the delivery-delay mechanism in earlier primary experiments.

For approach $j$ with $n_j$ available forecasts and signed error $e_{jk}=\widehat T_{jk}-T_j^*$ in minutes, we report
\[
 \operatorname{MAE}=\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\frac1{n_j}\sum_k|e_{jk}|,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{MSE}=\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\frac1{n_j}\sum_k e_{jk}^2.
\]
RMSE is the square root of that approach-weighted MSE; MSE has units of minutes squared. Coverage retains all scheduled cases in its denominator, including unavailable forecasts. Every arm has 100\% coverage in this representative screen. Accuracy metrics use available outputs and must always be read with coverage. Large-error percentiles pool clocks. Paired intervals resample vessels 1,000 times while retaining repeated approaches within each vessel; intervals are exploratory and unadjusted for 39 comparisons.

\subsection{How repeated reports affect results}
An earlier \texttt{march\_gate\_v1} experiment evaluated all 16 declared volume settings over the full 112-approach April cohort with five paired seeds. It separated affected share at fixed volume, exact/nonidentical biased repetition, total volume at fixed quality share, and reference-only repetition. In that earlier model version, B's MAE rose from approximately 6.41 to 6.83 minutes as affected share increased from 10\% to 50\%; C changed from 5.90 to 6.04. Fivefold nonidentical biased repetition changed B only from about 6.58 to 6.60 minutes. The measured repetition effect was small, not a dramatic failure.

The guard's bounded minute contributions explain a plausible resistance mechanism, but medians remain vulnerable when biased observations dominate a bin. Historical volume results do not establish robustness for the 2023-trained study: they are reported separately, excluded from the preliminary table above, and should be repeated with the final feature schema and repairs. The archived report and versioned configuration provide the audit trail.

\subsection{Where the approach still fails}
Missing-course repair lowers MAE while increasing MSE from 173.80 to 195.56 minutes squared, a 12.5\% increase. One retained approach illustrates why: at 12:10 UTC on 11 April, C's error on original records is $+141.97$ minutes. Masking course gives $+42.03$ without additional repairs, while reconstructing course gives $+143.28$. With course masking and noise together, the repair increases absolute error by 104.72 minutes. Restoring a plausible course can restore an existing model weakness. Removing this difficult approach from the official metric would conceal that risk.

The historical five-minute calibrated-envelope policy is also a negative result: it accepted zero C forecasts in the earlier model-version evaluation because its uncertainty bands were too broad. Those bands are not transferred to the current repair policy. Likewise, the current screen is one seed at selected severities; it does not validate all 256 on/off subsets, all slider values, opposite drift directions, or coordinated spoofing. Better fitted sensor values are not sufficient evidence of a better ETA.

\section{When a person should step in}
An operator should inspect the received timestamp, altered-field evidence, active warning, and available estimates before accepting an unreliable arrival-dependent plan. Simulator audit highlighting is for understanding the experiment and is not a deployable fault detector. A position inconsistency calls for checking an independent navigation or port source. The prototype exposes conditional estimates without overwriting the displayed received measurements; correction of a source report belongs to the accountable data provider or a separately verified input workflow. The interface does not implement a completed manual verification service or authorize the operator to edit ground truth.

The latest status policy separates an unavailable optional noise/drift reconstruction from an actionable review request. Turning or accelerating can invalidate a simple track fit without proving a fault. Such a case shows ``Estimate unavailable'' while preserving the input evidence. Detected inconsistencies, blocked corrections, missing required support, and other existing review warnings remain. This follows the general principle that alerts should prompt a useful human action.\footnote{\href{https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/}{Google SRE, Monitoring Distributed Systems.}}

Under the earlier status mapping, 400 of 448 original-record clocks requested review; 397 involved the speed-drift strategy, and 336 rejected a manoeuvring motion reference. A retrospective policy calculation retaining the other warnings reduces review labels to 74 of 448, or 16.5\%, with identical ETAs. This is evidence about label burden, not validated alert precision, measured operator time, or increased trust. The original forecasts can still be inaccurate without an alert. No representative operator study has been completed. A useful next walkthrough should separately record review, verification, correction, and escalation time, plus missed unreliable forecasts and unnecessary interruptions.

\section{Responsible use and limits}
The reviewed NOAA product is publicly accessible; its metadata lists coastal and ocean planning as the use constraint. Public access alone is not blanket authorization for redistribution or operational navigation. Retain attribution, preserve the selected product's metadata, and verify applicable terms before publishing raw subsets or acquiring a live feed. The repository keeps source CSVs, trained artifacts, and credentials outside version control; the site presents derived research evidence. Completing the 2023 provenance record and the March acquisition date remains a data-steward action.

MMSIs and precise historical tracks identify vessels and can expose operational patterns. We use identity only for grouping and display, without attempting to identify crew, enrich operator profiles, or join unrelated personal information. A live pilot would need explicit access, retention, logging, and sharing rules. Geography, receiving coverage, cargo/tanker selection, repeat vessels, and exclusion of unresolved approaches all constrain representation. Accuracy should be audited by vessel class, forecast horizon, data support, and previously seen vessels before broader claims.

For a proposed pilot, the data steward would own provenance and permitted use; the model lead would own versioned validation, drift monitoring, and rollback; an operations reviewer would own verification and escalation. The submitting team remains accountable for claims and source review. AI assistance was used for implementation and drafting. The evaluated predictor is the fitted regression/boosting pipeline; a language model is not part of the ETA inference path. This overview describes a research prototype, not a certified navigation aid, deployment approval, or a formal human-readiness rating.

\section{What comes next}
The present decision is \textbf{iterate}. There is evidence for selected noise and missing-field repairs, but combination recovery, large errors, and oversight are not ready for an operational pilot.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Model lead:} fit the final models on all eligible 2023 data, with time-ordered model selection and calibration confined to that year. Freeze the models and repair policy before the March 2024 test; evaluate its complete original clock schedule, multiple fault seeds, and source-volume controls. Investigate course-related failures using training-year development data and record any reconstructed-input augmentation. Preserve a further untouched period for confirming subsequent changes.
\item \textbf{Data steward:} supply and audit the full-year 2023 source manifest and final split counts, complete the March retrieval record, retain source/model hashes, verify redistribution terms, and obtain an independent position reference before making coordinate-recovery claims. The existing local four-thread CPU workflow supports another bounded offline study; no vehicle or live AIS access is required for that stage.
\item \textbf{Demo and operations reviewers:} run a short, explicitly exploratory oversight walkthrough, recording human effort separately from model latency. Follow the institution's required approval process before any participant study; a peer walkthrough alone cannot establish operator readiness.
\end{enumerate}
The checkpoint is a versioned report showing nominal/degraded accuracy, MSE and large-error behavior, output coverage, alert misses, review burden, and resource use on the new period. Revisit a controlled pilot only if the nominal-cost and degraded-gain goals hold without an unacceptable tail-error or workload tradeoff, and a port partner has approved data access, responsible roles, and non-actuating operating boundaries. Stop or narrow the scope if those conditions fail.
