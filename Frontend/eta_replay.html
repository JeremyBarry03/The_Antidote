A research prototype.
A practical question.

Can we keep arrival estimates useful when incoming data becomes stale or inconsistent—and make the need for verification visible?

What you’re looking at

A March 2024 replay of supported cargo and tanker approaches from NOAA Marine Cadastre AIS.

“Arrival” means crossing the analytical gate at −76.30° longitude, between 36.95° and 37.05° latitude. It is a crossing proxy, not verified terminal docking. Vessels and tracks fade for eight simulated minutes after that recorded event.

Map movement is smoothed between positions already received and can trail the latest message while completing a segment. The feed, ETA calculations, and scores use the original report values.

How the models and data are handled

Regression (A) and standard XGBoost (B) are frozen baselines. The Antidote (C) adds motion, freshness and consistency features, guarded input summaries, and training on simulated degraded feeds. Turning off the guard is an exploratory serving ablation; it does not retrain the model.

The actual dated partitions are used rather than an assumed 80/10/10 split: March 1–19 for training, March 20–24 for development, and March 25–31 for previously inspected exploratory work. April 1–7 calibrates intervals; April 8–21 is the protected evaluation period.

Limits, assumptions, and responsible use

This is a conditional arrival cohort, not every vessel in the harbor. Delay and position corruption are simulations; archive timestamps do not measure receiver latency or establish actual poisoning. Red field annotations are known injection truth for demonstration, not detected faults.

Warnings use observable report age and consistency. They can miss problems or raise false alarms. The fixed five-minute calibrated policy can defer all forecasts; this fleet view’s alerts are demonstrations, not an operational acceptance policy. Individual forecasts can still be badly wrong.

Public vessel identifiers and movement records are shown for research; no crew identities are needed. Confirm source terms, data-use review, and named data/model/operations owners before a pilot. No docking, safety, universal fairness, or measured savings claim is made.

What comes next

Validate the crossing reference and useful timing tolerance with a port reviewer, measure review effort, and assess a fresh held-out period. Report subgroup coverage and failure cases before considering operational use. The next step is iteration with those checks and accountable owners.
